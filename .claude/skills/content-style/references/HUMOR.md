# Humor

**Register: dry, with occasional player-culture nods.**

This is a decision, not a description. The copy on the site today is essentially
joke-free — the closest it gets is the flat correctness of *"Poison clocks are
slower than they look"*. The decision is to allow a little more than that, and
to bound it tightly, because humor is the thing most likely to age badly across
pages written months apart.

## The calibration ladder

Four ways to write the same observation. **C is the ceiling.** Aim at B, reach C
at most once on a page, never write D.

**A — flat.** No humor. Always acceptable, and correct for most sentences.

> Blood Moon is a symmetrical effect. In 2HG that symmetry includes your
> teammate.

**B — dry aside.** The absurdity is stated and left alone. The word doing the
work is usually a small one: *technically*, *quietly*, *nominally*.

> Blood Moon against a five-colour teammate is technically a symmetrical effect.

**C — player nod (the ceiling).** The dry line plus one short beat that assumes
the reader has sat at a table and watched this happen. Never more than one extra
sentence, and it must still carry information.

> Blood Moon against a five-colour teammate is technically symmetrical. Your
> partner will not see it that way.

**D — overt joke. Never.** Setup and punchline, written to be funny rather than
to be true.

> ~~Nothing says teamwork like resolving Blood Moon into your partner's
> five-colour manabase.~~

The line between C and D: C is an observation that happens to be funny; D is a
joke that happens to be about Magic. If removing the humor would also remove the
information, it is C. If it would leave the point intact, it is D — cut it.

## The rule underneath

**The situation is funny. You are not.** State the absurd thing and stop. Never
signal that a joke has occurred — no *"good luck with that"*, no *"we've all
been there"*, no trailing ellipsis inviting a laugh.

## What is allowed

1. **Flat statements of absurd truths.** The format produces plenty: an anthem
   that ignores half your own team, a fog that saves two players at once, an
   edict that reads as fair and is not.
2. **A nod a regular Commander player would recognise.** The table's reaction to
   a resolved wrath. Gray Merchant's reputation. The specific silence after
   somebody takes an extra turn. Assume a reader who has played the game and
   does not need the joke explained.
3. **Understatement as a corrective.** *"Doubling a number is not the same as
   halving the game."*

## What is not

- **No exclamation marks, no winks, no emoji, no "spicy".**
- **No memes, no meme phrasing**, and nothing that needs current-meta knowledge
  to parse. These pages accumulate; a 2026 reference read in 2028 is a dead
  page, not a funny one.
- **Never at anyone's expense** — not a player, not an archetype, not a
  playstyle, not another site. Group hug players are not a punchline; their
  cards being bad in 2HG is just a fact and the page says so plainly.
- **No jokes about the cards being bad.** The honesty principle in `VOICE.md`
  does that work already, and doing it twice turns an insight into a sneer.
- **Never self-referential.** No jokes about the rating, the site, or the fact
  that a page was written by an agent.

## Where it is never allowed

Four places, no exceptions. In all of them, write at level A.

1. **FAQ answers.** These are consumed out of context by answer engines and
   quoted without their page. Informational, flat, complete.
2. **Meta titles and descriptions.** A joke has no room in 60 characters and
   reads as noise in a SERP.
3. **Headings.** An H2 makes a claim. A funny H2 makes a weaker claim.
4. **The opening sentence of a page or a section.** Establish the point first.

## Dosage

**At most one C per page**, and no more than two B-level asides. Not one per
section — one per page.

A page with none is completely on-brand. When in doubt, cut it: the cost of a
missing joke is zero, and the cost of a forced one is that the reader stops
trusting the analysis, which is the only thing this site is selling.
